# Subscriptions (API and its web side)

- **Installments and reminders** (on `subscriptions`: `installments_total`, `remind_days_before`). **Installments:** `installments_total` (1 to 600, null = goes on) makes a plan with a fixed number of payments. `payments_made` is the count of **posted payments linked to it** (`counted()`, so a payment made by hand counts and a skipped, deleted planned one does not), `installments_left` is the rest (null when ongoing). `SubscriptionService.sync` sets the status to **`completed`** (a new `SubscriptionStatus`, never chosen in the web form unless it already is) once `payments_made >= installments_total`, trashes any leftover planned payment and plans nothing more; a skipped payment is not used up, the next cycle is planned and it still needs paying. Raising the total above what was paid, or clearing it with an explicit `null`, **reactivates** a completed plan in `update`; lowering it to what was paid completes it on the next look. A completed plan is left out of the active count and monthly cost, and the notification `installments_done` ("<name>: last payment made", once, under the payments switch) tells you. **Reminders:** `remind_days_before` (0 to 60, default 3, null = never) is how many days ahead of a payment its `payment_due` notification is made; **overdue is always told**. `NotificationService._payments` looks `MAX_REMIND_DAYS` (60) ahead and applies the subscription's own lead, and a planned payment that belongs to no subscription keeps the fixed `DUE_SOON_DAYS` (3). An explicit `remind_days_before: null` on create is kept empty on purpose (the column's default would otherwise fill it in). Web: `SubscriptionForm` has "Number of payments" and "Remind me" (`lib/subscription.ts`: `REMIND_OPTIONS`, `remindToField`, `installmentsText`), the table and cards show "3 of 12 paid", and `completed` has its own status chip. Not built: candidate detection and auto-record, email reminders.
- **Suggested subscriptions** (`app/subscription_detect.py`, `services/subscription_candidates.py`, `models/subscription_dismissal.py`, table `subscription_dismissals`; `GET /subscriptions/candidates`, `POST /subscriptions/candidates/accept` and `/dismiss`, declared before `/{subscription_id}`). **Worked out each time from the data, never stored**: it looks at posted, live, non-adjustment **expenses with a vendor and no subscription**, dated in the last 800 days (`WINDOW_DAYS`, at most 4,000 rows), grouped by **vendor and currency**, skipping a vendor that already has any subscription and a vendor-and-currency the user dismissed. `detect` (pure) judges **rhythm and amount separately**: the gaps must fit weekly (6 to 8 days, at least 4 payments, steady amount only), monthly (26 to 35, at least 3), every three months (84 to 98, at least 3) or yearly (350 to 380, at least 2) for at least 75% of them (**a gap of two cycles counts as one missed payment** for monthly and slower, not weekly); the last payment must be within one and a half cycles of today (a cancelled one is not suggested); amounts within 5% of the median are "the same", up to 40% is a bill that **varies** (monthly and slower only; the suggested amount is then the latest). Confidence is `high` (at least four payments, every gap in the band, a steady amount) or `medium`. **Nothing is created by looking**: `accept` finds the pattern again on the server (it takes only `vendor_id`, `currency` and the changes the person made, never a list of ids from the client), creates the subscription through `SubscriptionService.create` (the vendor, the most used non-archived account, method and category, the first payment's date as `start_date`, `amount_varies` from the pattern, the latest rate for a foreign currency) and **links every payment of the pattern to it** (`transactions.subscription_id`), so `last_paid_date` and `payments_made` are right and `sync` plans the next one in its place; 404 when the suggestion is gone. `dismiss` ("Not a subscription") is idempotent and kept in `subscription_dismissals` (an owned table, in backups; deleting the vendor removes it). **A notification** `subscription_candidate` (under the payments switch, `NotificationService._candidates`, key `subcand:<vendor id>:<currency>`, only the `high` ones, link `/subscriptions`) tells you once per vendor, so a loose guess never nags (and the email digest carries it). Web: `SubscriptionSuggestionsCard` (an accordion, closed by default, above the table on the Subscriptions page), `AcceptSuggestionDialog` (name, amount, every N unit, ready to change), `lib/subscription-candidates.ts` and the guide section "Suggested subscriptions". Not built: vendors without a vendor record (a charge typed with no vendor), detecting a price change as a new subscription, instalment plans (a fixed number of payments), and suggesting from planned transactions.
- **Subscriptions plan their next payment** (`SubscriptionService.sync`, `subscriptions.planned_through`, `subscriptions.account_id`). An active subscription keeps **one planned transaction** (an expense linked to it, in its amount, currency, category, vendor, method and optional account, description = its name) for the next billing date that is within `PLAN_AHEAD_DAYS` (35) of today, so planned transactions are the schedule: **Mark as paid** (the existing `POST /transactions/{id}/post`) posts it, `last_paid_date` moves on and the next cycle is planned in its place; **deleting a planned payment skips that cycle**, because `planned_through` (the last date one was made for) means the next one is always the cycle after it, so a skip never comes back. If a payment is made by hand for a cycle (a posted transaction linked to the subscription), the planned one for it is trashed. A cycle planned twice (a trashed plan restored after an edit replanned it, or two reads at once) keeps one plan: `sync` trashes the extra. One at a time: a late payment stays as the single overdue one and the next is planned only once it is paid or skipped. It is made by the daily `plan_subscriptions` job (00:20) and **on read** for one user (`GET /subscriptions`, a subscription's create/get/update through `_present_one`, `GET /transactions?planned=true` and `/transactions/ids?planned=true`), so the Upcoming list is right with no worker; `sync` is idempotent. Editing a subscription: a new `start_date`/interval or a status other than active trashes its live planned payments (and resets `planned_through`; resuming plans again), otherwise the planned ones follow only the fields that changed (an amount you adjusted on a planned one survives a rename). **Pay from** (`account_id`, optional, 422 if archived, someone else's, or in another currency than the subscription; picking one with no currency sent takes the account's currency and rate; `ON DELETE SET NULL`) is where the planned payments go, so posting one moves that account's balance. A planned payment counts toward nothing until posted (`counted()`/`reported()` as always). Web: `SubscriptionForm` has "Pay from" (it locks the currency to the account's), `UpcomingCard` is an **accordion, closed by default** (count, overdue count and the next payment in its header), a subscription's planned row says "Subscription" and its delete button reads "Skip".
- **Bills whose amount varies** (`subscriptions.amount_varies`, default false; `SubscriptionCreate/Update/Response`): a server, phone or utility bill that recurs but is never exactly the same, often in dollars. The subscription's `amount` is only the **usual** amount. Its planned payments are **estimates**: `AvailableBill.estimate` is true when the planned transaction's subscription varies, and the web shows them as "Estimate" with a leading "≈" on the Month plan (the button reads **Enter the real amount**), as "· estimate" in the dashboard card's working and as "about $10.00" in the Subscriptions list; nothing about how it is planned, paid or counted changes. **The real amount, rate or cost is entered when it is paid**: `PostPlanned` (`POST /transactions/{id}/post`) takes `exchange_rate` (the rate it was really paid at) **or** `base_amount` (what it really cost in the base currency, the rate is worked out as `base_amount / amount` to eight decimals), not both (422), and either is refused (422, nothing half done) for a bill already in the base currency; leaving both out keeps the planned rate, and a changed amount alone keeps it too. **The next payment is estimated at what the last one really came to** (`SubscriptionService.sync` reads the latest posted payment through `_last_payments` and copies its amount, rate and base amount, as long as it is in the subscription's currency); the usual amount is only the estimate until a first real payment. A bill that does not vary is planned at its amount again. Web: `MarkPaidDialog` takes `baseCurrency` (from `UpcomingCard` and the plan page) and for a bill in another currency shows **Exchange rate** (prefilled, with "Comes to MVR 184.80" worked out live by `baseOf`) and **Charged in MVR** (which disables the rate field), `payBody` in `lib/mark-paid.ts` decides what is sent (a rate equal to the planned one is left out), and `SubscriptionForm` has the switch "The amount changes from month to month". Not built: averaging or the highest of the last few as the estimate, and a per-payment history of how much a bill varied.
- `schedule.py` is pure subscription billing-date maths. Month and year steps clamp to month length but keep the anchor day.
- **Make a subscription from a transaction on iOS** (`Features/Subscriptions/FromTransaction/`): an expense that belongs to no subscription has **Make a subscription** in its detail sheet (`Transaction.canMakeSubscription`, as on Android). A name (the vendor's, else the category's; blank is left out so the server fills it), every N (1 to 120) weeks, months or years, then `POST /subscriptions/from-transaction/{id}`; the server copies the amount, currency, rate, vendor, method and category and the transaction's date is the first payment.
