# Accounts and cards (API and its web side)

- **Accounts are archived, not only deleted** (`archived_at`): `POST /accounts/{id}/archive` and `/unarchive`. An archived account leaves the default list (`?archived=true` lists them), the summary, the balance trend, and the account pickers; its transactions stay and still count in category, budget and monthly stats. New transactions and new goal links to it are refused (422), but a transaction already in it can still be edited (the web form keeps its account in the picker). `get` returns archived accounts too.
- **Default account:** `users.default_account_id` (`PATCH /users/me` with `{default_account_id}`, `null` clears it; the account must be yours and not archived). It is cleared when the account is archived and unlinked when it is deleted. The API never applies it: the web form starts a brand-new transaction in it, with the account's currency and suggested payment method. The cookie session only caches id, email and base currency, so pages that need the default read `/users/me` in their loader.
- **Bank and account number** on an account (`accounts.bank`, `accounts.account_number`, both optional) tell apart accounts that share a name (several savings accounts at one bank differ only by number) and are what a bank statement will be matched to (the number is in a statement's file name). `bank` is free text (the web form suggests the banks already used plus a short list, `BANKS` in `lib/accounts.ts`). The number is **normalised on the way in** (`normalize_account_number`: spaces removed, upper-cased, letters, digits, `*` and `-` only, at most 34 characters, so a masked card number like `533294XXXXXX4164` or an IBAN fits and a full card number is not encouraged); blank becomes null. **A user's accounts cannot share a number** (unique constraint `uq_accounts_user_account_number`, plus a friendly 409 from `_check_number` naming the account that has it); any number of accounts can have none, and other users are unaffected. The number is shown **in full, never masked** (chosen over last-4 masking), in the account cards, the account pickers (`AccountPicker`, the transaction filter) as `Name · Group · Currency · BML · 7730000486374`, and `AccountRef` (inside transactions and templates) carries both. The accounts search (`q`) also matches bank and number, ignoring spaces. They are in backups automatically and in the seed (made-up numbers).
- **Credit limit, exclude from stats and balance adjustment** (account polish). **Credit limit:** `accounts.credit_limit` (non-negative, in the account's currency) only for `CREDIT_LIMIT_GROUPS` (cards and overdrafts; a limit on another group is a 422, and moving an account out of those groups clears it). It **changes no balance**: you track a card as money owed (a negative balance), so the web just shows owed against the limit (`creditUsage` in `lib/accounts.ts`: a bar and percent on the account card, and a "Credit used" card on the dashboard when any account has a limit). **Exclude from stats:** `accounts.exclude_from_stats` leaves the account out of **everything that sums across accounts** (the account summary, net worth and the balance trend, which skip it like archived ones, and, through `Transaction.reported()`, the list totals, category totals, the budget's activity and the monthly stats), while its own balance, transactions, snapshots and its row in every list stay. **`Transaction.reported()` is `counted()` less balance adjustments and less excluded accounts, and is what any income or spending sum must use now**; `counted()` stays for balances, snapshots, goals, groups and subscriptions. `reported(include_excluded_accounts=True)` is used when the totals are filtered to one account, so looking at the excluded account itself still shows its figures. `AccountRef` carries `exclude_from_stats`, the transaction list marks such rows "Not in my totals", and the totals card says that excluded accounts and adjustments are not counted (`excludedNote`). **Balance adjustment:** `POST /accounts/{id}/adjust` with exactly one of `new_balance` (what the bank shows; the difference is worked out from the balance now) or `amount` (signed), an optional `date` (default today; earlier dates change the history from then on) and `note`. It records a transaction with **`adjustment=true`**: an income if the balance was too low, an expense if too high, in the account's currency at its rate, category "Balance adjustment" (`ADJUSTMENT_CATEGORY`), method other, description "Balance adjustment" unless a note is given. It moves the balance, the history and the snapshots (so it is stale-marked like any past transaction) but is **left out of every report** (`reported()`). **An adjustment is never edited** (a PATCH is a 422 saying to delete it and make a new one; bulk edit skips it), **cannot be refunded or put in a group**, can be trashed (which undoes it), and an archived account refuses one. Only this route makes them: `TransactionCreate` has no such flag. The web: a Tune button on the account card opens `AdjustBalanceDialog` (set to balance, or add or take away, with a live preview from `adjustmentPreview`), and an adjustment row shows an "Adjustment" tag with only a Delete button.
- **Account groups: a card is not an account.** An account is a place that holds a balance; a **debit card has none, it spends from its bank account**, so there is no "Debit Card" group any more (`AccountGroup` has no `debit_card`; the **payment method** `debit_card` stays and records how a transaction was paid). The migration `c9e4a2f7b165` moved existing debit-card accounts into `accounts`, and `BackupService` maps a legacy `debit_card` group in an older backup to `accounts` (`LEGACY_ACCOUNT_GROUPS`, before the value is decoded). A **credit card** (`card`) is the one card that is its own account (what you owe, with a credit limit). Web labels: `accounts` reads "Bank accounts" and `card` "Credit cards" (`ACCOUNT_GROUP_LABELS`); the account form shows `GROUP_HELP[group]` (`lib/accounts.ts`) under the group and name when a group is chosen; the guide `lib/docs/accounts.ts` ("Accounts, cards and groups") explains the model. An emergency fund is a **goal** that follows a savings account, not an account. **Cards** (`models/card.py`, `services/card.py`, `routers/card.py`, `/cards`, table `cards`, `transactions.card_id`) are how the model is expressed: a card is tied to one account. A **debit** card's `account` is the bank account it spends from (any group but a debt: `card`, `overdrafts`, `loan` are refused); a **credit** card's `account` is its own account in the `card` group and `repay_account` is the bank account it is repaid from (optional, never a debt; only credit cards have one). Only the **last four digits** are kept (`^\\d{4}$`), never a whole number. A transaction takes an optional `card_id` (**an expense only**: an income or transfer with one is a 422, and the web form shows the Card field only on an expense): the transaction then takes the card's account (`account_id` may be left out but must match, else 422 "belongs to another account") and, unless `payment_method` is given, the method of its kind (`TransactionService._card_method`); on update `card_id` set fills the account and method the same way, `null` clears it, moving a card transaction to another account is refused, and turning it into an income or a transfer drops the card. Deleting a card unlinks its transactions (`SET NULL`) and deleting an account deletes its cards; cards are in backups (`cards` is an owned table). The list, count, totals, ids, category totals and export filter by `card_id` (the shared `_conditions`), the export's Summary names it, and `TransactionResponse.card` is `{id, name, last_digits, kind}`. Web: `CardDialog` (add or edit on an account: the account decides the kind, a credit card also asks "Repaid from"), `AccountCards` on the account page (the cards, a **Repay** button that opens the transaction form as a transfer from the repayment account into the card account, `repaymentSeed` in `lib/cards.ts`), a **Card** field in the transaction form that only offers the chosen account's cards and, once picked, sets the account and payment method (`pickCard` in `useFormFields`; `stateFrom` in `request.ts` builds the request state), the card shown in a row's subtitle (`txSubtitle`, `cardText` "MIB Visa debit ••2815"), and a Card filter in the transaction filters (`card_id` in the URL, a chip, the export). The seed makes a debit card for BML Current and MIB Current and pays day-to-day spending with them. Credit card statements and due dates are built (next bullet). Not built: spending per card on a page of its own, cards in templates and in statement import, notifying when a card is close to its limit.
- **Credit card statement and due dates** (`accounts.statement_day`, `accounts.due_day`; `app/card_cycle.py`; `AccountService.statement` / `statements`; `GET /accounts/statements`, `GET /accounts/{id}/statement`; schema `CardStatement`). A credit card (group `card` only; a 422 anywhere else, and both days or neither, 1 to 31) says the day of the month its statement closes and the day the payment is due. **Nothing about a statement is stored, it is worked out each time** from the account's counted movements (`_movements`): the **statement balance** is what was owed at the end of the latest closing day (`last_close`: this month's day if it is today or past, else last month's; a day past a short month's end lands on its last day, `day_in`), **paid since** is the positive movements after that day (repayments, refunds, income), **remaining** = statement balance less paid since (never below zero), **due_on** = the first due day after the close (`due_date`), **spent_since** goes on the next statement, and **owed_now** is everything owed today. `state`: `none` (nothing was owed at the close), `paid`, `due`, `overdue` (past the due date with money left); `days_until_due` goes negative once late. A payment made before the close is already in the statement balance and is not counted twice; planned and trashed transactions count for nothing. Moving the account out of the card group clears both days; `statement_day: null` clears both. `GET /accounts/statements` lists every card in use with its days set, soonest due first (before `/{account_id}` so it is not an id). **Month plan:** `AvailableToSpend.card_payments` (`AvailableCardPayment`: account, date, amount in the base currency, overdue) lists the cards with `remaining > 0` due by the period end (overdue first) for accounts that count toward stats; **`available` = balance - bills - card payments - reserved**, and `card_payments_total` is separate from `bills_total`. Spending since the close is deliberately not in it (not due yet). A planned repayment transfer is not a bill, so nothing is counted twice. `money_short` merges bills and card payments in date order ("The money runs out at Visa card payment"). **Notifications** (under the payments switch, `NotificationService._cards`, only for cards with days set): `card_due` when money remains and the due date is within `DUE_SOON_DAYS` (3), `card_overdue` once late (keys `card-due:<account>:<close date>`, `card-overdue:...`, so each statement is told once), and `card_limit` when `owed_now` is at least `CARD_LIMIT_PERCENT` (90) of the limit (key `card-limit:<account>:<YYYY-MM>`, once a month); all link to the card's page. Web: the account form's two day fields for the Credit cards group (`statementDays` in `lib/accounts.ts` checks the pair in the browser), `CardStatementPanel` on the card's page (chip, "Pay MVR X by 12 Nov (in 4 days)", four figures, a **Repay** button that opens the repayment transfer already filled with what is left, `repaymentSeed(card, amount)`), `StatementLine` under each card on the Accounts list, a "Credit card statement" row with **Open the card** in the plan's Bills by day (`PlanRow.kind` is `card`, `cardId`; counted in the running figure and in "Bills to pay"), card statements in the dashboard card's working and `workingLine`, and the guide section "Statements and due dates" in `lib/docs/accounts.ts` (and the Month plan guide). Not built: a minimum payment, interest, a statement's own list of transactions, the near-limit notice for a card with no days set, and a credit card's autopay.
- **Account detail page** (`/accounts/$id`, `routes/_protected/accounts.$id.tsx`; the list moved to `accounts.index.tsx`, and each card's name links to the page; `components/account/*`, `lib/account-detail.ts`) shows one account: header (group, currency, bank and number), buttons (Add transaction with the account preselected, Edit account, Adjust balance; hidden when archived), tiles (balance or owed, money in and out this month in the base currency from `/transactions/totals` filtered to the account, credit use), a **Planned** box (upcoming transactions touching it, link to the month plan) and the account's own transactions with search, date range, type chips, paging, edit and delete. It needs no API of its own (`account_id` filter on the list; the list's `account_id` already matches a transfer's destination). **A row shows its effect on this account** (`accountEffect`): a transfer out is minus "To X", a transfer in is plus the received amount "From X", refunds are income; adjustments and refunds have no Edit. A bad id or another user's is the not-found screen. **Balance over time and a running balance** (read-only, no new table): `GET /accounts/{id}/balance-history?days=` (7 to 730, default 90) is the balance **at the end of each day** in the account's own currency, oldest first (`AccountService.balance_history`: the opening amount plus every counted movement up to that day, as `present` adds them up, so a transfer in is its `to_amount` and a planned or trashed one is not there; a movement dated later than today is left out of the chart though it is in the balance; a day with nothing repeats the one before), and `GET /accounts/{id}/balances-after?limit=` (default 500, at most 2000) is what the account held right after each of its newest transactions (`balances_after`, same-day order untimed before timed then by entry, so it ends at the balance). Both go through `_movements`. Web: `AccountBalanceChart` (an `AreaChart` card with 30 days / 90 days / 1 year chips, `days` in the URL, a caption of start, end, low and high, and the table toggle listing one row a week) and a **Balance** column on the account's rows (`balances` by id, `balanceMap`; rows older than the newest 500 show a dash; on a card it reads "… · Balance MVR 2,895.70"). `lib/account-detail.ts` holds `historyPoints`, `historyRows`, `historySummary`. The page has a Docs guide (`lib/docs/account-page.ts`, "An account's page"). Not built: the balance in the base currency for a foreign account.
- **Account trends** (`GET /accounts/trends?days=` (7 to 365, default 30), `AccountService.trends`, `schemas/account.py: AccountTrend`; web `components/charts/Sparkline.tsx`, `lib/sparkline.ts`, `components/account/AccountSpark.tsx`) draw the small balance line under each account's figure on the Accounts page, like the ones in a markets table. **One request for every active account** (an archived one has none), each `{account_id, currency, balances}` with one balance per day, oldest first, ending today, **in the account's own currency** and worked out by the same `balance_history` as the account page's chart (planned and trashed transactions count for nothing, a transfer moves both sides), so the curve and the figure agree. It is one `balance_history` per account (each reads that account's own movements), which is fine for a personal number of accounts; a single query for all of them is the next step if it ever matters. The server function **returns an empty list on any failure**, so the little charts can never break the page. `sparkline` (pure, tested) makes the path: the biggest number at the top, a flat series along the bottom (so every row shares a baseline), padding so the stroke and the end dot are not clipped; the component draws the line, a 10% wash and a dot for today, **green (`--chart-income`) when the balance rose over the period, orange (`--chart-expense`) when it fell, grey when it did not move**, and tells screen readers in words where it started and ended (never colour alone). The loader keys the trends by account id (`trendOf`). Not built: a percentage change next to it, other windows than 30 days in the page, and the same line in the account list of other pages.
- **Rate refresh and rate history** (`services/exchange_rate.py`: `cache_latest`, `refresh_rates`, `rate_history`; `accounts.auto_rate`). A foreign-currency account can **keep its rate at the market rate** (`auto_rate`, off by default, because a rate you typed is a rate you chose; 422 for an account in the base currency, and moving an account to the base currency switches it off). Turning it on (create or update) sets `exchange_rate` from the latest rate **at once** (`AccountService._follow_market`; if the source is unreachable the typed rate stays and the switch still saves), and the daily **`refresh_exchange_rates`** job (16:30 UTC, after the day's rates are out; `jobs.refresh_exchange_rates`) does it for every account that follows it. The job looks at every non-archived account not in its owner's base currency, makes **one lookup per (currency, base) pair** however many accounts share it, **keeps each rate under the day the source says it is for** (`ExchangeRate.requested_on = rate_date`, never twice) so history builds up even for accounts that do not follow the market, and a pair the source cannot answer is skipped for next time (the job never fails). It does not touch balance snapshots: past days keep their own rate, and the next midnight's snapshot picks the new one up. `GET /currencies/rate-history?base=&quote=&days=` (7 to 365, default 90) returns the rate on the days the source had one, oldest first: kept rates are used, and when fewer than a quarter of the days are kept the whole stretch is fetched once from the source and kept; if the source is down it serves what is kept (503 only when nothing is, 404 for a pair the source does not know). Web: the account form's "Keep this rate up to date" switch (under the rate field), a "Rate history" button on foreign-currency account cards that opens `RateHistoryDialog` (30 days / 90 days / 1 year, the market rate against the rate the account is valued at, low, high and movement, drawn with `AreaChart` whose `formatTick` shows decimals, `lib/rates.ts`), and " · auto" after the rate on the card. Not built: a rate history for transactions, other sources than Frankfurter.
- The currency list is fetched from Frankfurter and cached (only the codes). Market rates (`GET /currencies/rate?base=&quote=&on=`, `services/exchange_rate.py`) come from the same service and are only ever a **suggestion**: the web `RateField` pre-fills the rate for the transaction's date until the user types their own, and what is saved is always what is in the field, so a bank's real rate wins. Past days are cached in `exchange_rates` (a past day never changes); today and future days get the latest rate, uncached; a day with no data gets the nearest earlier one (`exact: false`). If the source is down the field just stays empty, as before.
