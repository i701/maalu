# Categories, tags and vendors (API and its web side)

- **Category colours and parents:** a category has a `color` (one of `ACCENT_COLORS` in `app/enums.py`, the same eight names as tags; a new one gets the least used automatically, or the one sent) and an optional `parent_id` (one level only: a parent cannot have a parent, a category with sub-categories cannot get one; `CategoryParentError` is a 422). A parent **includes its children**: filtering transactions by it, category totals, the export, and a budget line on it all use the family (`CategoryService.family`), and a budget line conflicts with a line on its parent or child. The parent is still usable on a transaction directly. The list is ordered parent then children; deleting a parent unlinks its children (`ON DELETE SET NULL`), and merging moves sub-categories. Responses carry `color`, `parent_id`, `parent`, `child_count`; transactions and split items carry `category_color`; `CategoryTotal` has `color`, `parent_id`, `parent`, `parent_color`.
- **Tags** (`/tags`, tables `tags`, `transaction_tags`, `template_tags`) are labels that cut across categories. A tag is unique per user by normalised name, has an automatic colour (one of `TAG_COLORS` in `models/tag.py`, the least used one first, named after the web accents), and belongs to the whole transaction, never to a split item. Requests send tag *names* (`tags: [...]`, at most 10) and the API finds-or-creates them, like categories; on update, `tags` replaces the list and `[]` or `null` clears it. Deleting a tag only unlinks it (it never blocks, unlike a category); `POST /tags/{id}/merge` moves everything to another tag without duplicates. iOS: More > Tags lists them with their colour and how many transactions and templates use each; + adds one, a tap renames it, a swipe deletes or merges (the shared `MergeSheet`, also used for vendors). The model is `TagRecord`, not `Tag`, which Swift Testing already names. Transactions, templates, the tag filter, the `q` search and the export's Tags column all know about them. The filter takes `tag_id` repeated plus `tag_match` (`any`, the default, or `all`), and a transaction with several of the chosen tags still counts once; a tag's `transaction_count` leaves out trashed transactions.
- **Vendor merge** (`POST /vendors/{id}/merge` with `{into_id}`) moves every transaction (trashed ones too), subscription and template to the target, copies what the target lacks (address, contact, currency, local or foreign, usual category, usual tags; what it has is kept), then deletes the source. Deleting a vendor, unlike merging, just unlinks its transactions.
- **Vendor defaults:** a vendor can carry a `default_category` and `default_tags` (by name in requests, found or created; `default_category: null` or `default_tags: []` clears them; deleting the category or a tag only clears the default, and merging a category or tag moves them). They are a convenience for the web form and the API never applies them itself: a transaction always says what it is. iOS: More > Vendors lists them (searching name, contact and address, filtered by Local, Foreign or Not set), with a form for the name, address, contact, local or foreign, currency, usual category and tags and **usual place** (the same location picker as a transaction's; a save sends every field, so a cleared one goes as null, and no place sends all three of its fields as null). A new transaction's form picks the vendor's place up when a known vendor is chosen and no location is marked yet (`TransactionFormModel.vendorChanged`), and a swipe to delete or merge.
- **The Salary category** (`categories.is_salary`, `SALARY_CATEGORY = "Salary"` in `app/enums.py`) is the one **default category**, for pay. Every account has it: made at registration (`UserService.register` → `CategoryService.ensure_salary`), made again on `GET /categories` when missing (data cleared, an old backup), and a category already called Salary is adopted (the migration flags existing ones; `get_or_create` flags a plain one it finds). It is created green and **does not count** in the colour rotation, so it never shifts the colours of the user's own categories. Rules, all 422 unless noted: **only income goes in it** (`TransactionService._check_salary` at the end of create and update, covering the transaction's category and every item's; bulk edit **skips** a spending row with the reason; subscriptions and non-income templates refuse it); **it is never a refund** (`_link_refund` refuses it, `GET .../refund-candidates` answers 422 "not a refund", `TransactionResponse.category_is_salary` lets the web hide the "Link to a purchase" button); **it is not half of a transfer** (the suggestions scan skips it and `link-transfer` refuses it); **it can't be renamed, put under a parent, hold sub-categories, merged away, or deleted** (409 for delete; its colour and kind can change, and another category can be merged *into* it, which keeps the flag). **A backup ignores it when deciding whether an account is empty and replaces it with the backup's own copy** (`BackupService._is_empty`, `restore`), otherwise every new account would refuse a restore. Tests that list categories filter it out with `if not c["is_salary"]`. Web: `canLinkRefund` is false for it, the Categories page shows a "Default · pay" chip, disables Delete and Merge on it and the form keeps its name read-only. Not built: the salary trigger that splits pay between accounts and goals (the next step, in the notes), a "Salary" flag for other income categories such as Bonus.
- **Spent and limit per category on the Categories page** (web only; iOS and Android do not have it, see `parity/features.json`). No API change: the page's loader asks `GET /transactions/categories` for the month picked (`on` in the URL, the first and last day from `monthBounds`; the same `MonthPicker` as the dashboard) and `GET /budget` for the period of that month. **Spent** (`CategorySpent`, `spendByCategory` in `lib/categories.ts`) is the month's expense total per category with refunds taken off; **a parent also gets its sub-categories' spending** (the tree is one level, and the transaction filter the figure links to counts the family the same way), and says "with sub-categories". The figure links to the transactions in that category and month; nothing spent is a dash. The header adds "MVR … spent in November" and "N% not sorted" pills. **Limit** (a column shown only while some limit runs, and a tag on the phone's cards): `limitFor` in `lib/budget.ts` finds the limit that lists the category, else the one that lists its parent (a limit on a parent covers its sub-categories, as `BudgetService` counts them), monthly limits before limits for set dates. **The ring is the limit's figure, not the category's** (a limit covers several categories; the tooltip names it: "Food limit: MVR 900 of MVR 1,800 (50%)"), so every category a limit covers shows the same ring. It is the shared `components/charts/Donut.tsx` (also the dashboard's Kept donut): a thin ring, rounded ends, the rest faint in the same colour, the percentage in the middle (the ring stops at full, the number keeps counting). Colour (`limitColor`): each limit takes a hue in turn, **the page's pink first**, then blue, violet, green, lime, slate (amber, orange and red are left out because they mean close), and **red once the limit is close to its level or over it** (status other than ok). Not built: the same on iOS and Android, a per-category budget (limits group categories), sorting the table by spent (the list is paged by the server).
- **Needs and wants** (`categories.kind`, `CategoryKind` in `app/enums.py`: `need` or `want`, null = **not sorted**). One label **per category**, chosen over a per-transaction one and over tags: the user thinks in categories ("food is a need"), and a sub-category already lets a child differ from its parent (Food = need, Food › Dining = want). `CategoryCreate`/`CategoryUpdate` take `kind` (explicit null clears it; leaving it out of an update keeps it), `CategoryResponse` has `kind` (its own) and `effective_kind` (its own, else its **parent's**). **`CategoryTotal.kind` and `CategoryMonthRow.kind` carry the effective kind**, so every report can split spending without another query (`TransactionService._parent_names` returns name, colour and kind of the parents). `PATCH /categories/kind` `{ids, kind}` (up to 500, declared before `/{category_id}`) sets many at once and answers `{updated}`, leaving out ids that are not yours. A merge gives the target the source's kind **only when the target has none**. Nothing infers a kind: new and old categories start unsorted, and `just seed` sets kinds for its categories (Software stays unsorted so the demo shows that bucket). A transaction or split item cannot override its category's kind (not built). Backups carry it automatically. Web: a "Need or want" select in `CategoryForm`, a Need/Want chip on each category (faded when inherited), a "Needs and wants (N not sorted)" button on the Categories page that opens `SortKindsDialog` (every category with a Need / Want / Not set switch, "only the ones not sorted yet", only what changed is saved; **a category with no kind of its own or its parent's is counted and listed as "not sorted" only once it has been used in a transaction**, `needsSorting` in `lib/needs-wants.ts`, because one that never was cannot move a figure; **a sub-category with no pick of its own shows "counts as Need/Want" under its name and its third button reads "Like parent"**, following the parent as picked in the dialog, so it never looks unsorted when it is not), and the dashboard's **Needs and wants card** (`NeedsWantsCard`, `lib/needs-wants.ts`: `kindShares` sums each kind exactly from the `/transactions/categories` totals the dashboard already loads, so it follows `reported()`; a kind at or below zero has no share; one bar of need in `--chart-line`, want in `--chart-expense` and a neutral for not sorted, always with names and figures, a table toggle and a link to sort the rest). **Filtering the transaction list by kind** (`GET /transactions` and its siblings take `kind=need|want|unsorted`, as `sorted_as` in `TransactionService`): it keeps the transactions whose category (or any split item's category) has that **effective** kind, intersected with the `category_id` family when both are given (`CategoryService.ids_of_kind`, `_family`); an empty match is an empty list, not "no filter". Web: the URL param is **`sorted_as`** (Trends already uses `kind` for Spending/Income), Need / Want / Not sorted chips in the filter panel, a "Kind: …" chip, and the export route maps it to `kind`. **A limit's kind is derived, not stored:** `BudgetLineResponse.kind` (and on fixed limits) is need or want when every category in the limit has that effective kind (`BudgetService._kinds`, `_line_kind`), else null; the web shows a chip on the card and, when any limit has a kind, `BudgetSummary` adds the limits up by kind (`budgetByKind`: spent against amount + carried + moved in - moved out, exact). **There is no per-purchase override, on purpose** (built and removed 2026-11-06): a category's kind is the only label, and a purchase that differs from it belongs in a sub-category (Food = need, Food › Treats = want), which keeps every figure and the filter reading one source. **The 50/30/20 rule** (web only, `lib/budget-rule.ts`, `components/dashboard/IncomeRule.tsx`, inside `NeedsWantsCard`): when the period has income, the card adds "Against 50/30/20 of your income": needs and wants (spending by effective kind) and **kept** as a share of income (`incomeRule`, from the same `/transactions/categories` totals as the rest of the card, so `reported()` rules apply), each with a bar, a tick at its target (`RULE_TARGETS`: needs up to 50, wants up to 30, kept at least 20), the figures and a status in words (`On target`, `Over target`, `Below target`, never colour alone). **Kept is income less all spending**, the money left over, not a transfer to a savings account (a transfer is not spending); it can be negative. Percentages are rounded to a tenth and the status compares the rounded figure, so exactly on target is on target. Spending in categories with no kind counts against kept but is not judged, and the card says how much. The table toggle lists the three rows with their targets. The targets are fixed (the rule's name); letting a person change them is not built. Not built: the rule on Compare or Trends, configurable targets. Android: a "Needs and wants (N not sorted)" button under the search on the Categories screen opens `SortKindsDialog` (every category except Salary with Need / Want / Not set, "only the ones not sorted yet", a sub-category left not set says it takes its parent's); it starts from each category's **own** kind (not the effective one), so only what changed is saved, one `PATCH /categories/kind` per kind (at most 500 ids each); the rules are `sortKindsButtonLabel`, `startingKinds`, `kindRequests` in `OrganiseLogic.kt`.
