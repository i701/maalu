# Transactions (API and its web side)

- **Deleting a transaction moves it to the trash** (`deleted_at`): `DELETE /transactions/{id}` sets it, `GET /transactions/trash` lists, `POST .../restore` brings it back, `DELETE .../permanent` removes it for good (only from the trash). The one exception to "trashed rows are invisible" is the category in-use check, which counts trashed rows because their `category_id` FK has no `ON DELETE`.
- **Time of day:** a transaction has `transaction_date` plus an optional `transaction_time` (a `Time` column, the clock as read on the wall, no time zone; `null` means unknown, and `PATCH` with `null` clears it). It only orders one day: lists sort by date, then time (newest first, soonest first for upcoming), with untimed ones after timed ones (`nulls_last()`), then by `created_at`. Filters stay on the date. The export has a Time column after Date. Templates, duplicates and "Add another" do not carry a time (the date resets, so the time would be stale). The web form has a Time field next to Date, and the lists show it after the date (`formatWhen` / `formatTime` in `lib/format.ts`).
- **Refunds** give money back for an earlier purchase. A refund is an **income** transaction with `refund_of_id` pointing at a posted, live **expense** (and, for a purchase with items, lines with `refund_of_split_id` naming the items given back); no new type. It is **money back for spending, not income**: wherever spending is summed, a refund takes away instead of adds. `Transaction.is_refund()`, `Transaction.spending()` (expenses and refunds), `Transaction.spending_base()` (signed SQL) and `spending_sign` (for the Python allocation loops) are what to use, in the same places `counted()` is: list totals (`income` leaves refunds out, `expense` is net, `refunded` says how much), category totals (a refund counts under its category's `expense`, negative), budget activity, monthly stats (counted in the month the refund arrives, so a month can go below zero), the export (Type "Refund") and the dashboard. Account balances still receive it (it is income to the account). A refund has the purchase's **currency** (and, unless typed, its rate), its **category** (forced; a purchase with items is refunded item by item, each line taking the item's name and category), and gives back **at most what is left** of the purchase (or of each item); refunds can be partial and repeated, planned ones reserve their share. The purchase side: `refunded_amount` (live refunds, in its currency) is a `column_property` on `Transaction` and on `TransactionSplit`; `refund_of` loads with `lazy="joined", join_depth=1` (`selectin` and `immediate` did not load it). A purchase **with live refunds keeps its amount, currency, category, items, type and posted state** (sending the same values back is fine, and unchanged items are kept so the links hold) and **cannot be trashed** until its refunds are (a bulk trash takes both together); **restoring a refund** needs its purchase live and room left (bulk restore does purchases first). A refund's type and category are fixed, it cannot move to another purchase, and `PATCH {refund_of_id: null}` turns it into plain income. **The other way round, existing income can be linked to a purchase** with `PATCH {refund_of_id: <purchase id>}` (`TransactionService._link_refund`, then the usual `_check_refund` at the end of `update`): only plain income (not an expense, transfer, adjustment, itself or something already a refund); the purchase must pass `_refund_original` (yours, posted, live, an expense); it keeps its own date, amount, account, rate, payment method and notes, takes the purchase's category (a different one is a 422), replaces its own items, and must be in the purchase's currency and within what is left; **a purchase with items needs `splits` naming the items** (each with `refund_of_split_id`, adding up to the amount). It then counts as spending given back instead of income everywhere refunds do. `GET /transactions/{id}/refund-candidates?q=&limit=` lists the purchases it could be for (same currency, on or before its date, with at least its amount left; the same amount first, then the same vendor, then the most recent; 422 for anything but plain income). Web: income rows have a link button ("Link to a purchase", `canLinkRefund`; it shares `refundOf` with the expense row's Refund button, `useRefundDialog` picking `{income}` or `{purchase}`) that opens `LinkRefundDialog`: a search over the candidates, and for a purchase with items the same item picker as a refund, which must add up to the deposit. Deleting a purchase for good unlinks its refunds. Bulk category changes skip refunds and refunded purchases. `app/seed.py` makes a few.
- **Templates** (`/templates`, table `transaction_templates`) are saved starting points: type, optional amount, currency, payment method, category, vendor, account (and `to_account` for a transfer), description. Category and vendor go in and out by *name* (found or created, like transactions), the exchange rate is deliberately not stored (the form suggests the market rate for the day), and every link is `ON DELETE SET NULL`, so a template never blocks deleting an account, vendor or category. A template with an account takes that account's currency; an archived account is refused. `PATCH` only renames; to change what a template fills in, delete it and save it again.
- **Bulk actions** work on up to 500 transaction ids per request and report instead of failing: every endpoint returns `{updated, skipped: [{id, reason}]}`, and an id that is not yours, not found or in the wrong state (live vs trashed) is just listed as skipped. `POST /transactions/bulk/trash`, `/bulk/restore` and `/bulk/purge` (purge only takes ones already in the trash) and `PATCH /transactions/bulk` (set `category`, `vendor_name` and `payment_method`, `add_tags`, `remove_tags`; at least one). A field that does not fit a transaction is left alone: transfers take only tags, and a split transaction keeps its items' categories. To act on everything a filter matches (all pages), `GET /transactions/ids` takes the same filters as the list and returns the matching ids (`?planned=true` for upcoming), and `GET /transactions/trash/ids` the trash's; both refuse with 422 past `MAX_SELECT_ALL` (5,000) rather than return a part. The bulk routes, `/ids` and `/trash/ids` are declared before `/{transaction_id}` so they aren't parsed as an id.
- **Attachments** (receipts: JPEG, PNG, WebP or PDF) on a transaction (`models/attachment.py`, `services/attachment.py`, `routers/attachment.py`, `app/storage.py`). The row (`transaction_attachments`: filename, `mime_type`, size) is the truth; the bytes are in storage under `{user_id}/{attachment_id}`. **Storage is a swappable interface** (`Storage`: put, get, delete, list): `S3Storage` (boto3 in a thread, path-style addressing, any S3-compatible store: AWS S3, Cloudflare R2, MinIO, floci in development) is used when `ATTACHMENTS_S3_BUCKET` is set (plus `..._ENDPOINT`, `..._REGION`, `..._ACCESS_KEY_ID`, `..._SECRET_ACCESS_KEY`), otherwise `LocalStorage` (a folder, `ATTACHMENTS_DIR`, `/data/attachments` in the image, a volume in `docker-compose.prod.yml`, which the API and the worker both mount). `just storage-up` starts floci and `python -m app.admin create-bucket` makes the bucket. Tests replace the store with `set_storage` (a temp folder, autouse in `test_attachments.py`), and `S3Storage` is tested against moto. **Upload goes through the API**: `POST /transactions/{id}/attachments?filename=` takes the file as the raw request body (not multipart; 10 MB, `ATTACHMENT_MAX_BYTES`; at most 10 per transaction), `GET /transactions/{id}/attachments`, `GET /attachments/{id}` (inline, `?download=true` forces a download; `nosniff`) and `DELETE /attachments/{id}`; another user's row or transaction is a 404. **The type is decided from the file's bytes** (`detect_mime_type`), never from a header, so an SVG or HTML file cannot be passed off as an image; filenames are cleaned. **The file is written first and the row second**, and a failed insert deletes the file; deleting removes the row, then the file, best effort. A trashed transaction keeps its files but takes no new ones. Hard-deleting a transaction, an account or a user cascades the rows only, so the daily `purge_orphan_attachments` job (04:30) lists storage and deletes files older than 24 hours with no row (anything not named like our keys is left alone); no other code path touches storage for cleanup. A transaction response carries `attachment_count` (a `column_property`, like `refunded_amount`). **Backups do not include attachments** (`transaction_attachments` is in `EXCLUDED`; the Backup card says so): back up the bucket or the volume with its own tools. Web: `AttachmentsField` and `useAttachments` in the transaction form (on a saved transaction files go up and come off at once; on a new one they wait in `pending` and upload after it is created, from `TransactionForm`'s submit), `lib/attachments.ts` (limits, `checkFile`, `uploadAll`), the browser reaches files through `routes/api.attachments.ts` and `routes/api.attachments.$id.ts` (both use `utils/proxy.ts`), and a paperclip shows on the row.
- **Web links on a transaction** (`models/link.py`, `services/link.py`, same router as attachments, `TransactionLink`) sit beside the files in the Attachments section: `GET` and `POST /transactions/{id}/links` (`{url, title?}`) and `DELETE /links/{id}`, at most ten per transaction (`LINKS_PER_TRANSACTION`). They are a **separate table**, not a kind of attachment, so that, unlike files, **links are in the backup** (`transaction_links` is an owned table). `normalize_url` is the one rule: trimmed, `https://` added when no scheme is typed, **http or https only** (`javascript:`, `data:`, `mailto:`, `ftp:`, `file:` are refused, and a bare `host:port` is still a host), a host with a dot (or `localhost`), no spaces, no embedded user name or password, at most 2000 characters. **Nothing on the server ever fetches a link** (no title lookup, so no SSRF); a link is only shown as `<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">`. A trashed transaction keeps its links but takes no new ones. A transaction response carries `link_count` next to `attachment_count`; the paperclip on a row counts both. Web: `useLinks` (same pending-until-saved behaviour as files), `LinkAdder` (not a `<form>`, because it sits inside the transaction form: Enter adds the link and is kept from submitting the transaction), `lib/links.ts` (`parseUrl` mirrors the API's rule), and after a new transaction's files and links go up the list is invalidated again so the paperclip appears. iOS: the new-transaction form holds files and links until it is saved (`TransactionFormModel.pending`, `pendingLinks`; what fails stays for Save to retry), and a saved transaction's own screen lists them (`TransactionFilesSection`): a file opens with Quick Look after downloading through `GET /attachments/{id}`, a link opens in the browser, a swipe removes either, and new ones go up at once (none on a trashed one); `ParsedLink` mirrors `parseUrl`.
- **Transaction groups** (`models/group.py`, `services/group.py`, `routers/group.py`, table `transaction_groups`) bundle a handful of related transactions under a name and show what they add up to (a trip over several accounts, instalments, a treatment bought over weeks, an expense plus what a friend paid back). **A group changes nothing else**: the transactions keep their category, tags and amounts and every other total ignores groups. The link is a nullable `transactions.group_id` (`ON DELETE SET NULL`), so **a transaction is in at most one group** and deleting a group only unlinks; `Transaction.group` loads with the row and `TransactionResponse.group` is `{id, name}`. **A group holds 2 to 50** (`MIN_GROUP` / `MAX_GROUP`): creating needs two to fifty ids, adding stops at fifty, and **a group left with fewer than two is deleted**, both when transactions are taken out (`POST /groups/{id}/transactions/remove` answers `{removed, dissolved}`) and when one is deleted for good or turned into a transfer (`GroupService.dissolve_undersized`, called from `TransactionService.delete`, `bulk_purge` and `update`). A member must be yours, **not in the trash, not a transfer and not in another group** (a 422 saying which); a transfer that a member is edited into leaves its group. The summary (`GroupResponse`: `transaction_count`, `date_from`, `date_to`, `money_in`, `money_out`, `net`, base `currency`) counts **live** transactions for the count and dates, and **only `counted()` ones** (posted, not planned) for the money, in `base_amount`; income is money in (a refund is income, so it lowers the net) and expenses money out; a trashed member stays linked and comes back counted on restore. Routes: `GET/POST /groups` (list newest activity first, `q` over name and note, `X-Total-Count`), `GET /groups/{id}` (summary plus its transactions as `TransactionResponse`s), `PATCH` (name, note; `note: null` clears), `DELETE`, `POST /groups/{id}/transactions` (add). **There is no transaction-list filter by group** (it would need threading through six routes): the group dialog lists its members instead. Groups are in backups (`transaction_groups` is an owned table; ids are remapped like everything else) and the seed does not make one. Web: a "Group" button in the selection bar (`GroupDialog`: a new group, or add to an existing one; needs 2 to 50 selected to create), a blue group chip on each row that opens `/groups?group=<id>`, the Groups page (`routes/_protected/groups.tsx`, in the Organise nav group, icon `inventory_2`) and `GroupDetailDialog` (summary, members each with a take-out button, edit, delete). `folder_open` is **not** in the bundled icon font; check any new Symbol name against it. iOS: More > Groups (`GroupsView`; the list has no row selection yet, so a group is made there: a name, a note and the transactions ticked in `TransactionPickerView`, the newest 50 or a search, with transfers and members of another group shown but not choosable), the group's own screen (summary, members with a swipe to take one out, Add Transactions, Edit, Delete; it closes when the group is gone, deleted or dissolved), and a Group line on a transaction's screen.
- **Linking two transactions into one transfer** (`models/transfer_dismissal.py`, `services/transfer_link.py`, `routers/transfer_link.py`, table `transfer_dismissals`) fixes a move of your own money entered twice (an expense in one account and an income in another). `GET /transactions/transfer-suggestions` scans for pairs: same amount and currency, different accounts, within `SUGGEST_WINDOW` (2 days), both live, posted, not adjustments and with an account; the expense has no items or refunds and the income is not a refund; each row is in at most one suggestion (closest wins). **The scan only suggests; nothing merges until the user confirms.** `POST /transactions/link-transfer {expense_id, income_id}` turns the **expense row into the transfer** (keeps its date, amount, account and notes; `to_account` and `to_amount` come from the income; category, vendor and subscription are dropped like any edit into a transfer; tags of both are kept; the income's description is used only when the expense has none) and **trashes the income** (so undoing is a restore plus an edit). Balances do not change; income and expense totals drop by the pair. `POST .../dismiss` ("Not a pair") remembers a pair in `transfer_dismissals` (idempotent; in backups). The routes are mounted before the transaction router. A cross-currency pair can only be linked by hand (the suggestion needs the same currency). **`GET /transactions/{id}/transfer-candidates`** (`TransferLinkService.candidates`, mirrors `refund-candidates`) lists the entries one transaction could be the other half of: the opposite side, **exactly the same amount and currency**, a different account, any date, passing `_check_pair`, a dismissed pair left out, closest date first (`limit` 1 to 50); an empty list means no merge should be offered, 422 for a transfer or anything that can never be a side, 404 for someone else's row. Android asks it when a detail sheet opens and shows "Merge with N matching entries" only when the list is not empty, and the picker is exactly that list (the app no longer searches). Web: `TransferSuggestionsCard` (accordion, closed by default, above the list), and a "Make a transfer" button in the selection bar when `linkablePair` (`lib/transfer-link.ts`) finds exactly one expense and one income in different accounts, with `MakeTransferDialog` to confirm.
- **Locations and the Places map** (`transactions.latitude`, `longitude`, `place`; the same three on `vendors`; `services/transaction.py: places`). A transaction can be **marked on the map**: both coordinates (`Numeric(9,6)`, strings in JSON, within +-90 and +-180) or neither, and an optional name (`place`) that needs them. The create schema validates the pair; on update an explicit null removes one, so `TransactionService._check_location` checks the combined result (one coordinate alone is a 422, a blank name becomes none). **Marking a spot looks nothing up by itself**: you drop the pin; only the optional place search (below) asks a geocoder, and only when you press Search. A **vendor has a usual place** with the same three fields (clearing one coordinate clears the whole spot); like the other vendor defaults the API never applies it, the transaction form fills it when a known vendor is picked and no location is marked yet; merging vendors keeps a place the target lacks. `GET /transactions/places?date_from&date_to` (before `/{transaction_id}`) answers **where spending went**: expenses with a location, through `reported()` (so no transfers, adjustments, excluded accounts, planned or trashed) with refunds taken off (`spending_base`), in the base currency, grouped by **radius clustering** (a point within `SPOT_DEGREES`, about 100 m, of a spot's running middle joins it; a fixed grid was tried first and split a spot that straddled a cell edge), the spot's name being the one most of its transactions gave, biggest spend first. Templates, imports and the export do not carry a location; a duplicate and "Add another" keep it. Backups carry it automatically. Web: **Leaflet** (`useLeafletMap` loads it only in the browser, when the map's element mounts, and builds the map with the server's tile source), `LocationDialog` (click the map, drag the pin, type or paste `lat, lng`, "Where I am", an optional name; saving with no pin removes the location), `LocationField` (the "Add location" button or the chip, in the transaction form and the vendor form), the **Places page** (`routes/_protected/places.tsx`, Money group, ranges this month, 3 months, this year, all time; `PlacesMap` draws a circle per spot sized by spend, with a popup built from text nodes because names are typed by people, and the list below it focuses a spot) and a map-pin chip on transaction rows. iOS: More > Places draws the same spots on **Apple's MapKit** (the SwiftUI `Map`; no key, token or tile server, so `MAP_TILE_URL` does not apply, and Apple serves the map), with the web's four ranges, a circle per spot that keeps its size on screen and grows by area with spend (`PlacesModel.diameter`), and the list below it; tapping a circle or a row focuses that spot. Every iOS map (Places, the location picker, a transaction's small map) has the same corner buttons (`MapButtons`): **Earth View / Map View** switches MapKit's `hybrid` and `standard` styles, one choice for all of them remembered on the device (`MapLook`, `@AppStorage`), and **Full Screen** opens the same map over the whole screen sharing its camera (a transaction's still map becomes movable there), where the button closes it. **Tiles are the operator's choice**: `MAP_TILE_URL` and `MAP_ATTRIBUTION` (web environment; `.env.example`, `docker-compose.prod.yml`) default to OpenStreetMap's public server, which asks for light personal use, and the browser fetches the tiles itself, so that server learns which area is being looked at; set your own tile server to avoid that. `getMapConfigFn` reads them in the `_protected` loader and `MapConfigProvider` hands them to the maps. Leaflet writes colours as SVG attributes that cannot hold `var()`, so the marks take them from classes in `styles/map.css`, which also flips the light tiles in dark mode. `just seed` marks purchases from the local vendors around Malé. **Place search** (`GET /places/search?q=&near_lat=&near_lng=&limit=`, `routers/place.py`, `services/geocode.py`, `PlaceSearch` in the picker): looks a place up by name through a geocoder (`GEOCODER_URL`, default Nominatim's public server; **empty turns it off**, then the route answers 404 "Place search is not set up" and the picker says so). It is **the one thing besides map tiles that sends something a user typed to another service**, so it is narrow: only the typed text (tidied) and roughly where the map is (a bias `viewbox`, `bounded=0`, so it prefers nearby places but hides none) go out, **only when Search or Enter is pressed, never as you type**; it sends the identifying `GEOCODER_USER_AGENT`, **spaces requests at least 1.1 s apart** (`_pace`, Nominatim's policy is one a second), caches an identical search for an hour (200 entries, keyed by the lower-cased words, the area and the limit; a failure is not cached), and is **limited to 20 searches a minute per person** (`rate_limit.check_place_search`, 429). The browser never calls the geocoder: the API does it, so the User-Agent and pacing hold whoever asks. Results are the short name (the geocoder's `name`, else the first part of its address), the full address and coordinates at six decimals, rows without usable coordinates are dropped, an unreachable geocoder is a 503. Choosing a result puts the pin there, zooms in and names the spot after it unless a name is already typed. iOS: the transaction form's Location row opens `LocationPickerView` (MapKit: tap the map to drop the pin, Where I Am through `CLLocationUpdate` with a when-in-use `CLServiceSession`, asked only on that button, the coordinates typed or pasted, an optional name, and place search through `GET /places/search` only on Search, a 404 saying it is not set up); the spot is sent as six-decimal strings (`TransactionLocation`), all three fields together, and an edit that removes it sends three nulls; a copy and Save and add another keep it, and a transaction's screen shows it on a small still map. Not on iOS: filling the spot from a vendor's usual place (the iOS form applies no vendor defaults yet). Not built: clustering at low zoom, a location filter on the transaction list, a location in the export.
- **Items on iOS** (`Features/Transactions/Form/Items/`, a port of Android's `SplitLogic.kt`, itself the web's `lib/splits.ts`): the form's **Split into items** switch (not on a transfer) gives each item a name, quantity, price each and category, with GST none, included or excluded at a typed rate; a line is `quantity × price` (3 and 4 decimals, half up), GST taken out (`base × rate / (100 + rate)`) or added on, and the amount shown is the items' total (`TransactionDraft.effectiveAmount`), the category row hidden (the API takes the biggest item's; the request sends no `category` with `splits`). A saved item keeps its exact line total and tax (`PurchaseItemDraft.lockedAmount`) until its quantity or price is edited; editing a purchase with items reads the GST rate back from its first taxed item. Turning the switch off on an edit sends `splits: null`. Purchases with items are editable on iOS now (refunds and balance adjustments still are not), and a transaction's screen lists its items.
- **Refunds on iOS** (`Features/Transactions/Refund/`, a port of Android's `RefundLogic.kt`). In a transaction's detail sheet an expense with room left has **Refund** (`RefundFormView`: an amount up to what is left, or for a purchase with items a switch and an amount per item; the date, an optional time, the rate when the currency is not the base one, an account in the purchase's currency, a note), a refund has **Edit refund** (its items are locked, the amounts change; a blank time, note or account is sent as `null`), and plain non-Salary income has **Link to a purchase** (`LinkRefundView`: `GET /transactions/{id}/refund-candidates?q=`, and for a purchase with items the ticked amounts must add up to the money that came back). The detail shows "Refund of …" on a refund and "Refunded …" or "Fully refunded" on the purchase. Not on iOS: "after refunds" on the list totals.
